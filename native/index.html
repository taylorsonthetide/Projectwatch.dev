Ride comparison · any vessel

Compare wave rhythm at different speeds for a chosen heading. Start with your boat’s waterline length; hull type alone does not supply its pitching or rolling response.

When enabled, waterline length and heading are required before saving. Leave this off to save your weather limits only.

Use speeds through the water, within your boat’s normal operating range. Heading is an input for comparison, not a suggested course.

Only enter a rhythm you have found comfortable on this boat in comparable head or bow seas. Without it, we show the calculation but do not invent a best speed.

An observed roll period flags possible rhythm conflicts; it is not a complete stability assessment. Hull type is saved as context; hull, stabiliser, loading and planing-transition effects need measured vessel-response data and are not guessed. A saved comfortable rhythm applies only to comparable conditions and the same operating regime.

About your comparison

Wave height is significant wave height, not the largest individual wave. Period is the mean interval between waves. Wind is modelled at 10 m above the surface.

Green means the available forecast is within your settings. Amber means conditions approach a limit or waves have a short period. Red means a limit is exceeded, or near-limit waves combine with a short period. Grey means data is missing, stale or incomplete.

These are your comfort preferences, not a measure of your boat’s capability. Check current local conditions, official warnings, tide and harbour advice before deciding to go.
