Who gives way when two boats cross?

When two power-driven vessels are crossing in sight of one another with risk of collision, the vessel with the other on its starboard side must keep out of the way. If circumstances allow, it should avoid crossing ahead.

Start with the situation

Starboard is your right-hand side when facing the bow; port is your left. This crossing explanation is for power-driven vessels. Overtaking, narrow channels, restricted visibility and vessels with special responsibilities can require different rules.

What should the give-way vessel do?

Take early, substantial action to keep well clear. Check that the action is working and continue checking until the other vessel is past and clear. A small, uncertain change can be difficult for the other skipper to recognise.

Does stand-on mean a right to carry on?

No. The stand-on vessel initially keeps course and speed. It may act when it becomes apparent that the give-way vessel is not taking appropriate action. It must act when the give-way vessel's action alone cannot avoid collision. Keeping a proper lookout remains essential.

What if the boats are head-on or overtaking?

  • Head-on: two power-driven vessels on reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses with collision risk both alter to starboard and pass port-to-port.
  • Overtaking: the overtaking vessel keeps out of the way until finally past and clear. A later bearing change does not remove that duty.
