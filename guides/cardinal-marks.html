How do cardinal marks work?

A cardinal mark tells you which compass side of the mark has navigable water: north of a north cardinal, east of an east cardinal, south of a south cardinal and west of a west cardinal.

Use a compass direction, not left or right

The instruction stays the same whichever way your boat is facing. If you approach a north cardinal from the west, you still need to identify the water to its north. Check the chart for the hazard, your position and the depth needed for your boat.

Recognise the four marks

Cardinal mark recognition by day and night
MarkBlack and yellow bands, top to bottomTwo black conesWhite light
NorthBlack, yellowBoth point upContinuous quick or very quick
EastBlack, yellow, blackPoints away from each otherGroups of 3 quick or very quick flashes
SouthYellow, blackBoth point downGroups of 6 quick or very quick flashes, then a long flash
WestYellow, black, yellowPoints towards each otherGroups of 9 quick or very quick flashes

A useful night-time memory aid

Think of a clock: east is three, south is six and west is nine. Remember the extra long flash for south. North flashes continuously. Read the charted characteristic for the complete rhythm and period.

Cardinal or lateral?

Lateral marks identify channel sides using the direction of buoyage. Cardinal marks use compass directions. Learn both systems rather than applying the red-and-green rule to every buoy.
